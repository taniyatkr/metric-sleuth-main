# Methodology

## The company

**Wavemark** is a fictional company invented for this project. It's modeled
on the real, publicly-known business shape of usage-based CPaaS providers —
companies that sell communications APIs (SMS, voice, email, phone
verification) to other businesses, billed by consumption rather than by
seat. Wavemark is not any specific real company, and everything below is
synthetic: there is no real dataset behind this project, unlike the
project's earlier iteration (which used the real IBM Telco Customer Churn
dataset — replaced entirely by this one, because a flat-rate telecom
subscription snapshot couldn't produce a real sales pipeline or genuine
usage-based expansion/contraction, and a custom build could).

Every number below was generated by two scripts in `mcp_server/` —
`generate_dataset.py` (pipeline, accounts, channel pricing) and
`generate_usage_and_tickets.py` (monthly usage, support tickets) — using
`random.seed(42)` and `random.seed(99)` respectively, so the dataset is
reproducible. Nothing here is hand-edited after generation.

## Tables

| Table | What it is |
|---|---|
| `opportunities` | The B2B sales pipeline — every deal Wavemark's sales team has ever worked, won, or lost. |
| `accounts` | Wavemark's customers — one row per **won** opportunity. |
| `channel_rates` | List price per unit for each API channel (reference table). |
| `monthly_usage` | Realized, consumption-based revenue: one row per account, per month, per channel actually used. |
| `support_tickets` | Fully synthetic support-ticket history: biased toward accounts heading toward cancellation generally, plus a concentrated wave tied to one specific planted incident (below). |

### `opportunities`

`opportunity_id, segment, source, rep, created_date, qualified_date,
qualified_amount, stage, close_date, closed_amount, is_won`

- `qualified_date` / `qualified_amount` are null for the ~15% of
  opportunities that never reach the Qualification stage.
- `closed_amount` is the deal size **at close**, separate from
  `qualified_amount` (the size estimated back when it was qualified) —
  real deals shrink or grow between the two, modeled here as a random
  ±30% "slip."
- A slice of recently-created opportunities are still genuinely open
  (`close_date`/`is_won` null) as of the reference month, so pipeline
  health — not just closed history — is a real, queryable thing.

### `accounts`

`account_id, opportunity_id, company_name, segment, industry, signup_date,
primary_channel, starting_mrr`

`company_name` and `industry` are generated, not sourced — synthetic
company names built from word-combination lists, assigned a plausible
CPaaS-customer industry (logistics, fintech, healthcare, etc.).

### `monthly_usage`

`account_id, month, channel, units, revenue`

This is the table that fixes a real limitation from the project's earlier
Telco-based iteration: because usage genuinely rises and falls month to
month (instead of one flat `monthly_charges` value held constant for a
customer's whole tenure), **expansion and contraction are real, queryable
signals in this data** — not something the source is silent on.

### `support_tickets`

`ticket_id, account_id, opened_date, category, priority, resolved`

Exactly the same pattern as the earlier Telco-based project's
`support_tickets` table: fully synthetic, documented as such, and
deliberately biased — not to fake a conclusion, but to encode a realistic
underlying pattern (accounts in trouble file more tickets, of higher
priority and more cancellation-flavored categories, in the months right
before they leave) for `gtm-intelligence-investigator` to actually
discover via its own querying and joins, rather than being handed one.

## The planted incident: a March 2026 Mid-Market SMS billing disruption

Neither `accounts` nor `monthly_usage` nor `support_tickets` has a column
that says so anywhere — this is documented here as an answer key, the same
way the general churn/ticket correlation above is documented as synthetic
rather than exposed as a flag in the schema.

**What happened, as scripted in `generate_usage_and_tickets.py`:** in
March 2026, every Mid-Market account whose primary channel is SMS, and who
was already a customer before then, takes an immediate revenue hit that
month, a lingering extra decline for three months after, and a 22% chance
of fully cancelling within 1-3 months of it — including accounts whose
underlying trajectory had them stable or growing beforehand. The same
accounts also generate a wave of `billing_dispute` tickets dated
March-April 2026, skewed toward `high`/`urgent` priority. 32 accounts were
affected in total.

**Important correction, caught while building the Investigator's staged
playbooks:** this was originally documented (and scripted) as "Wavemark
raised SMS pricing." That claim doesn't hold up: `channel_rates` is a
static table — `list_price_usd` never varies by time, for any channel,
anywhere in this dataset. There is no mechanism by which a real price
change could exist here, and a direct check confirms it — SMS's implied
price (revenue ÷ units) is exactly `0.0075` in every single month from
2023 through 2026, including March. The incident is mechanically a
**demand-side usage/revenue shock plus a `billing_dispute` ticket wave**,
not an actual rate change. An investigation that concludes "Wavemark
raised prices" is asserting more than the data can prove; the honest,
fully-supported conclusion is "a billing dispute incident concentrated in
Mid-Market SMS accounts caused a sustained usage pullback culminating in
churn for some accounts," with the *cause* of the disputes implied by the
ticket category and timing rather than provable from a revenue-per-unit
calculation, since that calculation cannot move in this dataset no matter
which channel or period it's run against. `gtm-intelligence-investigator`'s
question 1 playbook has a dedicated stage (channel-level units-vs-revenue
comparison) that exists specifically to confirm this rather than assume
it — see `gtm-intelligence-investigator/retention_drop_playbook.py`.

**Verified with real SQL** — monthly realized revenue, Mid-Market only,
split into the affected slice (SMS-primary) vs. the rest of the segment:

| Month | Affected slice (Mid-Market, SMS) | Rest of Mid-Market |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-01 | $151,919 | $165,358 |
| 2026-02 | $150,015 | $169,145 |
| 2026-03 | $120,819 | $166,486 |
| 2026-04 | $114,694 | $164,990 |
| 2026-05 | $109,624 | $160,978 |
| 2026-06 | $97,386 | $167,466 |
| 2026-08 | $101,651 | $175,196 |

The affected slice breaks from the rest of the segment exactly at March
2026 and never recovers; the rest of Mid-Market stays flat-to-growing
across the same window.

**Ticket volume tells the same story, on the same timeline** — `billing_dispute`
tickets by month, same two groups:

| Month | Affected slice: billing_dispute (of total tickets) | Rest of segment: billing_dispute (of total) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-01 | 0 (of 3) | 0 (of 5) |
| 2026-02 | — | 2 (of 8) |
| 2026-03 | **41 (of 42)** | 0 (of 4) |
| 2026-04 | **29 (of 29)** | 0 (of 5) |
| 2026-05 | 0 (of 1) | 0 (of 4) |

Both the revenue break and the ticket spike land in the same segment, the
same channel, and the same two months — discoverable by grouping
`monthly_usage` and `support_tickets` by segment/channel/month and
noticing where they move together, not by reading a flag.

## Calibration sources (cited, not invented)

- **Sales cycle length by deal size, and overall B2B win rate (~20-21%,
  trending toward ~19% in 2025):** Gradient.works,
  ["2025 B2B Sales Performance Benchmarks"](https://www.gradient.works/blog/2025-b2b-sales-performance-benchmarks)
- **Segment ACV bands (SMB / Mid-Market / Enterprise) and GRR/NRR ranges
  by segment:** Bessemer Venture Partners' State of the Cloud data, as
  summarized in [SubJolt's 2026 NRR/GRR benchmarks guide](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/nrr-grr-benchmarks/)
- **Usage-based vs. seat-based NRR (108% vs. 98%):** Benchmarkit's
  "2026 SaaS and AI-Native Metrics" report, also via the SubJolt summary
  above.

**Where sources disagree:** m3ter's own benchmarks put usage-based NRR
noticeably higher — 115–130%+ — than Benchmarkit's 108%. We calibrated to
Benchmarkit's figure as the primary blended target because it reads as the
more representative "typical" number, with m3ter's range treated as a
plausible best-in-class ceiling rather than the target itself. Both are
named here rather than picking one silently.

**Segment-level win rate is our own assumption, not a citation.** Neither
source breaks out win rate by segment — only the overall blended rate and
cycle length by deal size are sourced. We assumed SMB deals convert more
often than Enterprise ones (a common, plausible pattern — lower friction,
shorter cycles, less competitive scrutiny) and tuned the split (SMB 25%,
Mid-Market 18%, Enterprise 12%, each as a share of opportunities that
reach Qualification) so the **blended** rate lands on the sourced ~20-21%
figure. Stated plainly as an assumption.

**Month-by-month usage trajectory parameters (growth rates, decline
windows, churn probability shares by segment) are also our own choices,**
not sourced — the benchmarks above describe the *aggregate outcome*
(retention rates), not the mechanics that should produce it. We picked
parameters, generated the data, and then verified the actual result with
real SQL rather than assuming the targets were hit — see below.

## What we actually measured (verified with real SQL, not asserted)

**Pipeline**, all 1,400 generated opportunities:

| Segment | Win rate | Avg. sales cycle |
|---|---|---|
| SMB | 24.8% | 22.2 days (source range: 14-30) |
| Mid-Market | 18.3% | 59.8 days (source range: 30-90) |
| Enterprise | 10.4% | 150.9 days (source range: 91-210) |
| **Blended** | **21.7%** | — |

All three cycle-length figures land inside the sourced ranges. The blended
win rate (21.7%) matches the sourced ~20-21% figure closely; Enterprise's
individual rate (10.4%, target 12%) is a bit low, likely sampling noise
from a small won-deal count (11 enterprise wins total) rather than a
calibration problem.

**Retention — two windows, deliberately, because one of them is supposed
to look worse:** every figure below is computed fresh from raw
`monthly_usage` rows — no churn flag or retention number is stored
anywhere in the schema.

*Baseline, before the incident (Feb 2025 cohort → Feb 2026, entirely
before the March 2026 hike):*

| Segment | NRR (target) | GRR (target) |
|---|---|---|
| SMB | 86.6% (80-100%) | 76.5% (70-80%) |
| Mid-Market | 90.4% (90-120%) | 80.1% (80-90%) |
| Enterprise | 110.5% (>100%) | 93.0% (>90%) |
| **Blended** | **95.8%** | **83.3%** |

Every segment lands inside its Bessemer-sourced target band, and Mid-Market
sits right at the low edge of both its bands (90.4% / 80.1%) — a
legitimate, unforced result of the calibration, not a coincidence created
for the incident. This is the "normal" retention picture: what
`gtm-metrics-explorer`/`gtm-intelligence-investigator` should see for any
period that doesn't include the incident.

*Trailing 12 months ending Aug 2026 (Sept 2025 cohort → Aug 2026), which
does include it:*

| Segment | NRR | GRR |
|---|---|---|
| SMB | 89.9% | 78.6% |
| Mid-Market | **76.4%** | **70.3%** |
| Enterprise | 118.2% | 98.8% |
| **Blended** | 92.9% | 81.4% |

SMB and Enterprise barely move. Mid-Market drops roughly 14 points of NRR
and 10 points of GRR from its own baseline — that gap is the incident,
and it's real: not a targeting miss, a deliberately engineered before/after
so there's something for `gtm-metrics-explorer` to report a dip on and
`gtm-intelligence-investigator` to actually explain.

**Support tickets vs. churn**, verified by joining `support_tickets` to
account status (churned = no usage in the most recent month after having
had usage before):

- Churned accounts average **3.84 tickets** each vs. **1.08** for accounts
  still active — roughly 3.6x.
- Churned-account tickets skew sharply toward `high`/`urgent` priority
  (68% of their tickets) vs. active accounts (40%).
- Churned-account tickets skew far more toward `cancellation_request`
  (22% of their tickets vs. 3% for active accounts).
- `billing_dispute` alone is **not** a clean churn signal in this dataset
  by itself (~30% of tickets in both groups) — because the incident wave
  of billing disputes hit Mid-Market/SMS accounts regardless of whether
  they ultimately churned. It only becomes a sharp signal once narrowed to
  segment + channel + month, which is exactly the incident section above.
  Worth stating plainly: a category alone can mislead; the same category
  concentrated in a specific slice and window is the real story.

This is a real, queryable pattern in the data — exactly the kind of signal
`gtm-intelligence-investigator` is meant to find on its own, through
`run_query` and joins across `accounts`, `monthly_usage`, and
`support_tickets` — not a number we're asserting without having actually
run the query.
